# Road to A1

## Syllables

I am currently using [dutchgrammar.com](dutchgrammar.com) for learning the absolute basics. For starters, I am going to start with dividing a word into syllables.

>[!summary] Syllabification
>
>A word is divided into syllables by its vowels. If two vowels are pronounced as one (_ij_), we consider it one vowel. When put together with one or more consonants, every combination becomes a syllable.

There are **four rules** which apply to most Dutch words:

1. If two vowels are separated by one consonant, the consonant forms the beginning of the second syllable.

|Word|Syllables|Meaning|
|---|---|---|
|moeten|moe-ten|_must, have to_|
|maken|ma-ken|_to make_|
|zeuren|zeu-ren|_to whine, to moan_|

2. If the vowels are separated by more than one consonant, the first syllable gets one consonant, and the second gets the rest of the consonants:

| Word      | Syllables   | Meaning          |
| --------- | ----------- | ---------------- |
| paarden   | paar-den    | _horses_         |
| dingen    | din-gen     | _things_         |
| wennen    | wen-nen     | _to get used to_ |
| venster   | ven-ster    | _window_         |
| enclave   | en-cla-ve   | _enclave_        |
| obstinaat | ob-sti-naat | _stubborn_       |

3. A compound word consists of two or more separate words. We split the compound word at the boundary between the original words, leaving each original word intact. We do the same for words derived from nouns or verbs: _vergeetachtig = vergeet + achtig_ (forgetful = forget + -ish).

| Word        | Syllables     | Meaning               | Incorrect            |
| ----------- | ------------- | --------------------- | -------------------- |
| waarom      | waar-om       | _why_ (de ce)         | not: _waa-rom_       |
| meeteenheid | meet-een-heid | _unit of measurement_ | not: _mee-teen-heid_ |
| broodoven   | brood-o-ven   | _bread oven_          | not: _broo-do-ven_   |
| koopakte    | koop-ak-te    | _sales contract_      | not: _koo-pak-te_    |
| huurauto    | huur-au-to    | _rental car_          | not: _huu-rau-to_    |

> I see that it holds for pronunciation as well. I pronounce each individual word and the final one simply makes sense.

4. "Ease of pronunciation": This is what we generally do, but if the second syllable begins with a consonant cluster that's hard to pronounce, we put one (or more, if needed) of those consonants at the end of the preceding syllable.

* In Dutch, a syllable never begins with two identical consonants (see last row).

|Word|Syllables|Meaning|Incorrect|
|---|---|---|---|
|koortsig|koort-sig|_feverish_|not: koortsig|
|ambtenaar|amb-te-naar|_civil servant_|not: am-bte-naar|
|Delftse|Delft-se|_from Delft_|not: Del-ftse/Delf-tse|
|erwten|erw-ten|_peas_|not: er-wten|
|startten|start-ten|_(we) started_|not: star-tten|

### The combination 'ch'

There is one consonant-combination that does not abide by the four syllable rules described before: **ch**. This combination is never separated. It is another way of writing the letter _g_.* For this reason, we regard is as a single consonant. The words below are therefore not separated by three consonants (_c, h_ and _t_) but by two (_ch_ and _t_ or _r_ and _ch)_.

* self note (e acel hhh cand tragi o flegma)

| Word      | Syllables   | Meaning          |
| --------- | ----------- | ---------------- |
| lachen    | la-chen     | _to laugh_       |
| richel    | ri-chel     | _ridge_          |
| zuchten   | zuch-ten    | _to sigh_        |
| krachten  | krach-ten   | _forces, energy_ |
| rechter   | rech-ter    | _judge_          |
| monarchie | mo-nar-chie | _monarchy_       |

And of course, observing the 4th rule: if _ch_ is enveloped by consonants (e.g. 'rcht'), we place _ch_ at the end of the preceding syllable (like with koortsig, ambtenaar, etc.). If we do not do this, the second syllable starts with _ch_ + another consonant (which will generally make it hard to pronounce).

|Word|Syllables|Meaning|Incorrect|
|---|---|---|---|
|burchten|burch-ten|_castles_|not: bur-chten|
